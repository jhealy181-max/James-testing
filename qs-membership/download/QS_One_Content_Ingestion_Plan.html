Turning QS newsletters and the magazine into QS One content

Read each newsletter issue and magazine edition once, split it into self-contained items, tag every item by QS One component, theme, region and role, and serve it through one curated feed. Each new source is a configured connector, not new code.

Principle: ingest once, split into items, tag against one controlled vocabulary, have editors review what the machine is unsure of, then curate per person
Newsletters → partsEach issue is broken into its sections: lead feature, in-brief items, chart, data point, people moves, event promo. Each part goes to the component it serves.
Magazine → articlesEach edition is broken into articles: cover story, briefing, opinion, profile, Spotlight. Each is tagged by theme and grouped into collections.
Tag with confidenceRules handle what's obvious (series, section headings, source tags). An AI classifier does the rest against a fixed taxonomy. Anything below a confidence threshold goes to an editor.
Curate, don't floodThe feed ranks by role, interests, region and recency. Editors can pin and build collections, and a "why you see this" line explains each item.

The pipeline

Seven stages. Each can be re-run independently, records what it did, and skips work it has already done for the same content version.

1DiscoverFind new or changed issues via API, RSS, sitemap or the edition Cover page

3ParseRemove page furniture; extract title, date, author, series, body, images and links
4SplitBreak newsletters into parts using series templates; one magazine page becomes one article

6ReviewEditors check low-confidence and sensitive items, set access tier and labels
7PublishIndex for search, related items and the curated feed; build collections

Splitting newsletters into component parts

An issue is a bundle. QS One needs the parts: a lead feature for Intelligence, a discussion question for a Community, a chart for Pulse context, an appointment for Connect.

How splitting works

Series templates
A small configuration file per series names its recurring sections (e.g. Mid Week Brief: teaser questions → lead feature → "In brief" items → promos) and the heading text or layout that marks each one.
Generic fallback
Where no template matches, split on headings and dividers, then merge fragments under about 60 words into their neighbour.
Part types
Lead feature · News brief · Data point · Chart · Discussion question · Case example · People move · Event · Promo · Sponsored.
Promos are kept
Event and magazine promos are not deleted. They become links to the matching summit or edition, and never appear as articles in the feed.
Provenance
Every part keeps its parent issue, its position, the original link (anchored to the section where possible) and the issue date.

Magazine: articles, themes and collections

Each article becomes one item with a type, themes, regions, institutions and people, and joins edition, theme and editor-made collections.

Edition index
Read the edition's Cover page to list its articles, their order and the cover story. The sitemap catches anything not listed.
Per article
Title, standfirst, author, article type, publication date, body, pull quotes, images with credits, and links.

1 · Rules first

Rules are cheap and predictable. The series sets defaults (Chart of the Day → Chart → Pulse), section headings set the part type, and source tags such as Ghost's "Student Flows" map to themes.

2 · AI classifier

A Claude model returns structured output against the current taxonomy: themes, regions, audience, a 40-word summary, a "so what" line, and a confidence score for each tag. Each item is classified once per content version.

3 · People on exceptions

Items go to the editor queue if confidence is below 0.7, or if they are sponsored, name individuals, or may be breaking news. Every edit becomes a labelled example that improves the rules and prompt.

Quality is measured, not assumed. Editors hand-tag a set of about 200 items. Every change to the prompt or taxonomy is scored against that set (agreement on component, theme and type) before it goes live. Changing the taxonomy triggers a background re-tag of affected items.

The curated feed

One ranked list per person, assembled from three inputs: what the person cares about, what is fresh, and what editors want seen. Each item carries a plain reason it was chosen.

Ranking signals

Relevance
Theme matches the person's interests; audience matches their role; region matches their regions or their institution's markets.
Context
A boost for items on the theme of their next community session or the Pulse they just answered.
Freshness
Decays over days for news briefs and over weeks for features and frameworks.
Editorial
Pins, collections and suppression (e.g. retracted or superseded items).
Diversity
At most two items in a row from one source or theme; one item per story where the newsletter and magazine cover the same thing.
Access
Tier-locked items show as a teaser with "Included from Member". Partner items only appear in labelled slots.

Making it scale

The proof of concept stores everything in one JSON file. That is fine for a demo, but the archive (years of newsletters, 46+ magazine editions) and daily updates need a proper store and background jobs.

On Replit

Database
Replit PostgreSQL with full-text search, plus vector search for "related items" and duplicate detection.
Raw store
Replit Object Storage for original pages, keyed by fingerprint.
Jobs
A job table in the database with retries and back-off, worked by a separate scheduled process so ingestion never slows the app.
Secrets
Anthropic API key and the newsletter platform key, held in Replit Secrets.
Dependencies
This relaxes the "no runtime dependencies" rule. It needs a database driver and an HTML parser.

Design choices that keep it cheap and safe

Incremental
Only new or changed content is processed (API timestamps, ETags, fingerprints).
Idempotent
Re-running a stage on the same version does nothing new, so failures can simply be retried.
Backfill in batches
The archive is classified through the batch API overnight; live items go through as they arrive.
Model choice
A small, fast model for tagging; a stronger model only for summaries that editors will publish.
Observability
Each source shows last run, items found, items queued for review and errors on the existing "QS data sources" page.

Rights and governance

Owned, but check contributors

QS owns both titles. Guest authors, partner Spotlights and image credits may carry their own terms. Display mode per item: full text, excerpt with link, or link only.

Labelling stays strict

Opinion is labelled as opinion. Sponsored content is labelled Partner. Nothing from a newsletter becomes "QS Analysis" without editorial approval.

People and institutions

Power Moves contains named individuals. Use public information only, link to the source, and never feed it into partner targeting.

Delivery plan

Prove it on recent content, then add the archive, then scale out to other sources.

Phase 0 · 1 week

Access and taxonomy

Confirm both platforms and get first-party access (newsletter API key; magazine export or API). Agree the theme taxonomy and display rights. Hand-tag 200 items as the quality benchmark.

Phase 1 · 2–3 weeks

Recent content in the app

Connectors, parsing and splitting for the last 3 months of newsletters and the last 3 editions. Rule-based tags. Items appear in Intelligence with component and theme filters. Runs inside the current proof of concept.

Phase 2 · 3–4 weeks

Classifier, review, feed

AI tagging with confidence scores; an editor review queue in the team view; collections; curated feed; context panels on Pulse and Community pages. Move to Postgres and background jobs.

Phase 3 · ongoing

Archive and new sources

Batch-tag the full archive; related items and duplicate detection; automatic Weekly Signal draft; add HolonIQ, qs.com insights, summit agendas and webinar transcripts as connectors.

Decisions needed from you

Five answers unlock Phase 0.

1 · Access route
Can the QS digital team give QS One an API key for the newsletter platform and an export or API for the magazine? Recommended: yes, because it is more reliable than reading pages and avoids being blocked.
2 · Display rights
Show full text in QS One, or excerpts linking back to newsletters.qs.com and magazine.qs.com? Recommended: full text for QS-authored items, excerpt for guest and partner items.
3 · Taxonomy owner
Who in editorial owns the theme list and the review queue (about 1–2 hours a day at launch)?
4 · Access tiers
Is newsletter and magazine content free in QS One (it is public today), with only QS One-specific analysis tier-locked? Recommended: yes. It helps the free Network grow.
5 · AI use
Approve using a Claude model for tagging and summaries, with editor approval required before anything is published as QS Analysis.
