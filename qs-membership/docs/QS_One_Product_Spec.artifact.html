QS One product specification

A decision network university leaders renew because it shapes business-critical decisions. It has four components with one job each, sits on one tier ladder, and launches first for student recruitment and international offices.

How the components reinforce each other

Communities sit at the centre of the loop

Each component feeds the next. Moving from one-off roundtables to standing communities matters most, because it creates the return visits and the practice no one else holds.

Pulse measures

Members answer a short monthly survey and get live peer operating data before official statistics.

Intelligence interprets

QS analysts turn Pulse results and QS data into a weekly Signal and monthly briefings.

Communities solve

Peers work the implications through, sharing use cases and joint projects.

Connect introduces

Active members are visible and get matched to peers, TNE hosts and partners, including at QS summits.

Pulse wave closes→Signal the same week→Community read-out session→Use case published→Introductions and summit meetings→Next wave

Users and their needs

Leaders must make fast, high-impact decisions on lagging, generic data. The most useful knowledge sits with peers.

Why existing data falls short

1 · Data lags

Visa approvals and industry benchmarks arrive months late, so trends are hard to see as they unfold.

2 · Data is generic

Published data is not framed around the practical decisions of higher education or of a specific country.

3 · Experience sits with peers

Real experience shared through communities and structured case studies beats generic advice or data.

Component specifications

Each component has one job, a defined MVP scope, clear access rules by tier, and a measure of whether it is working. MVP marks what ships for January 2027.

Monthly domain briefing

Component 2CommunitiesSolve. From one-off roundtables to sustained work, building on the legacy of Reimagine Education.ValuePeers who return month after month build trust and produce practice no one else holds.CostMost expensive to run: each community needs a facilitator.
Structure
MVPFour domain communities plus regional chapters aligned to QS summits. Launch with Student Recruitment & International Office; add others as facilitators are hired.
Each community has
A named QS facilitator, a published charter, member commitments (e.g. answer the monthly Pulse, bring one live decision per session) and regular outputs: use cases, frameworks and joint projects.
Sessions
MVPMonthly online sessions (Chatham House), plus meet-ups at QS summits. Open quarterly sessions for Free members, including partner-hosted sessions, which are labelled.
Discussion

Access
Free: open quarterly sessions. Member: one domain community plus a regional chapter. Member Plus: all domain communities. Council: Executive Council seats, consulted on community themes.
Measure
Members attending two or more sessions a quarter; outputs published per quarter; share of members posting.
Component 3PulseMeasures. Rapid visibility on what decision-makers are doing, nationally, regionally or globally.ValueLive peer operating data between official releases: an early signal on trends such as shifts in TNE focus.RuleOnly contributors see results.
Recruitment Pulse
MVPMonthly wave. Change in deposits vs last year by source market; change in visa refusal rate; biggest risk; TNE plans. About 3 minutes; one response per institution; editable until close.
Role benchmarks
LaterQuarterly surveys targeted by role: AI adoption (digital), financial resilience (CFO), employer partnerships (careers).
Results view
Your values vs the peer median by market (amber vs grey bars); the middle half of peers; median last wave; four status cards: strongest and weakest vs peers, most improved and largest decline since last wave.
Cohorts
Free: all respondents (headline). Member and Member Plus: by region and institution type. Council: a bespoke benchmark cohort chosen by the institution (minimum five).
Safeguards
Any group under five institutions is hidden. No named student data or institution-specific pricing. Pulse data is never an input to QS rankings. Legal review of question design before launch.
Measure
At least 25% of institutions answer each Pulse; time from wave close to Signal; repeat contributors.

Introductions

Platform requirements

One institution, one set-up. What the strategy calls density depends on removing today's separate points of contact and product onboarding.

Identity and access

Sign-in
MyQS single sign-on. QS One appears in the MyQS app switcher alongside Rankings Preview.
Entitlement
Held by the institution, not the seat. Unlimited named colleagues; each has their own role family, interests and contact settings.
Roles
QS One lead (edits the institution profile, invites colleagues), member, commercial partner, QS coordinator.
Rules
Every access rule is enforced in the service, not only in the interface. Council is capped at 25.

Onboarding and activation

Single checklist
Name a lead · verification by QS · complete the institution profile · two or more colleagues active · first Pulse answered · join a community (or open session).
Active institution
Two or more members, each logging in at least every two months, and at least one survey answered.
Invitations
The lead invites colleagues; MyQS users at ranked institutions can be offered role-based access from April 2027.
Packaging
Free Network access can be bundled with existing QS products and summit passes to seed the network.

Content and data

QS content
Public qs.com pages read on a schedule with robots.txt checks; short excerpts and links only; live, stale or saved status shown. Move to approved feeds from QS data owners.
Pulse store
Responses held per institution, per wave; aggregation service applies the minimum group size to every cut.
Integrations
MyQS (identity), CRM (contracts and entitlements), events platform (summits, passes, attendance), email platform (Signal, notifications), analytics products (Market Expert, Student Insight, WUR Rankings).
Entities
Institution, Person, Plan, Community, Session, Post, Output, Intelligence item, Pulse, Wave, Response, Introduction, Event, Board post, Audit event.

Trust and governance

Independence
Membership, tier and participation have no bearing on QS rankings, surveys or methodology.
Consent
No contact without opt-in; introductions need both sides; partners never see Pulse results or discussions.
Labelling
QS Evidence · QS Analysis · Member practice · Partner, on every item.
Audit
An activity log of joins, contributions, introductions and plan changes, for coordinators.

Commercial partners

Upside, not part of the £5m target. Every component has an opening, but network growth comes first.

IntelligenceSponsor a labelled webinar series; co-develop research
CommunitiesHost an open session on a problem the partner solves
PulseAggregated insight on what leaders prioritise (groups of 5+)
ConnectMeetings with members who opt in; the partner board
Timing is an open question. Recommendation: introduce labelled partner sessions and the board once the launch community has 40+ active institutions, so partners do not arrive before density.

Open items

Questions from the strategy, plus points in it to reconcile before the November decision.

Strategic questions

Go to market
How QS One links to existing sales and packages, e.g. free Network or Member access bundled with conference passes and sponsorships.
Content mix
Balance of member-generated content and QS-led thought leadership; recommend QS-led at launch, member use cases growing through the facilitator pipeline.
Partnerships
If and when to include sponsorships (see the recommendation above).
Resourcing
How much existing editorial, events and analyst capacity can be reused versus new facilitators.

Points to reconcile in the strategy

Target date
The summary says £5m by June 2028; the table heading says "end 2028". The spec assumes June 2028.
Member count
40 new in 2027 plus 100 in 2028 gives 140, but 130 are paying at the end, which implies about 10 churned. Make churn explicit.
Total paid
50 + 140 new + 20 existing = 210, against 200 paying. This is consistent only if churn is assumed.
Currency
Tiers are in US dollars ($20k/$60k/$110k); the walk is in sterling at $1.33 (£15k/£45k/£85k ≈ $20k/$60k/$113k). Choose one list currency.
Allowances
Member Plus introductions and passes ("8?") are not fixed. The proof of concept assumes 6 introductions a quarter and 8 passes.
Free directory
"Directory for inbound connections" is read as: Free members can be found and receive introductions, but cannot request them.
