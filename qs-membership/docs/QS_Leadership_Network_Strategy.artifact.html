01 · Executive summary

The answer on one page

The proposition

QS Leadership Network is a verified, year-round network of university decision-makers. It puts QS evidence, peer data and QS convening into one member workspace. Institutions pay for it because it helps with live decisions. Partners pay for it because it is the most credible way to build relationships with the leaders who make those decisions.

  1. Don't sell content. AI is making analysis and summaries close to free, and seat-based "insight" is the most exposed way to price information (Gartner fell 50% in 2025). The things that will stay scarce are verified people, data members contribute, trusted evidence with a clear source, and the room. The membership is built on those four.
  2. Two paying sides, one network. Institutional subscriptions priced for the whole institution fund the core. High-value corporate partnerships pay for credible, opt-in access to the network, not for audience data. Governments and university systems can buy for groups of institutions.
  3. Members produce value as well as consume it. Circles (curated peer networks), member-written practice notes and give-to-get "Pulse" benchmarks turn members into contributors. This is the embryo of the contributory data rail set out in the Authority Strategy.
  4. Summits become the heartbeat, not one-off transactions. Each QS summit becomes a peak in a continuous cycle: matchmaking before, closed roundtables and live Pulse during, digests and Circles after. Delegates convert into members, and one-off sponsors convert into annual partners.
  5. Independence is part of the product. A published Independence Charter separates membership, partners and consulting from the rankings. When the ranked party pays, the ranking is exposed, and governance is QS's licence to operate.
  6. Start narrow, prove, then scale. A founding cohort of 15–20 institutions and 3 founding partners, launched at the APAC and Middle East summits in November 2026, with a go/no-go at Global Skills Week in March 2027. Illustrative scale at year 3 is about £8m in annual recurring revenue.

03 · The proposition

A decision network, not a content subscription

The existing Strategic Intelligence proof of concept got the domains, journeys and guardrails right. The shift is in where the value sits and who pays.

From: a strategy portal

  • A library of briefings and links; value depends on how much we publish
  • Paid per institution, mostly for access to content
  • Peer contact limited to a directory and drafts
  • Events listed as links to the calendar
  • One paying side (universities)
  • Engagement is reading
→

To: the leadership network

  • A verified graph of decision-makers; value grows with every member
  • Priced for the whole institution by depth of participation: Circles, benchmarks, the room
  • Curated Circles, concierge introductions and private networks
  • Summits built into a year-round cycle, including matchmaking and roundtables
  • Two paying sides: institutions and corporate partners, plus government and system buyers
  • Engagement is contribution: Pulse data, practice notes, Q&A
For institutions

"Decide with evidence and with peers who have done it."

Role-tailored QS intelligence, benchmarks you can only see by contributing, and trusted counterparts facing the same decision.

For partners

"Earn the trust of the leaders who decide."

Credible, opt-in, well-governed access to university leaders through content, roundtables and problem-led collaboration, all year round.

For QS

"Turn episodic reach into recurring relationships."

A single member graph that feeds summits, data products, consulting pipeline and, over time, the contributory data rail.

05 · The offering

Seven modules in three layers

The four existing domains stay as the organising spine: Global talent & mobility · Institutions & reputation · Skills, jobs & outcomes · Innovation & new models. Every module is tagged to them.

Layer 1 · ConnectThe network

① Leader Profiles & Directory

Verified institution and individual profiles: priorities, challenges and what each member is open to. Peer discovery by shared challenge, with concierge introductions that require both sides' consent.

All tiers

② Circles

Bespoke networks. Domain Circles are open working groups. Peer Circles are curated cohorts of 8–12 institutions working on a shared challenge. Regional Chapters are aligned to the summit regions. Private Networks are hosted spaces for alliances and consortia.

Leadership tier and above
Layer 2 · KnowIntelligence & data

③ Intelligence

Central QS content: role-tailored Signals, monthly executive briefings, data stories from QS datasets (e.g. Global Student Flows), frameworks and summit digests. Every item shows its source and date.

All tiers

④ Member Exchange

Content written by members: practice notes, case examples, "Ask the network" Q&A, session notes. Only verified people can post, and moderation follows the Chatham House rule where requested.

All tiers

⑤ Pulse Benchmarks

Give-to-get peer data. Members answer short operational pulses (e.g. deposits by source market, stage of AI adoption) and see aggregated results only after contributing. Results are suppressed below a minimum cohort size.

Contribute: all · Cohort cuts: Leadership+
Layer 3 · Meet & transactConvening & partners

⑥ Convene

Summit matchmaking, member lounges, closed-door roundtables, virtual roundtables between summits and an annual Members' Forum. Includes a pool of summit passes.

Pass pool: Leadership+

⑦ Partner Hub

Verified partner profiles, clearly labelled partner briefings, partner-hosted roundtables and a Challenge Board where institutions post problems and partners propose pilots. Members choose whether they can be contacted.

Partners · members opt in
How it links to QS data products: the membership doesn't give away licensed datasets. It is their shop window and way in. Data stories and Pulse show what the evidence can do, and the next step (a Student Recruitment Dataset, AI Capability Assessment or consulting project) is a clear, separate purchase.
06 · Content model

Central QS evidence and member-written content, with trust built into both

The mix matters. Content from QS provides the authority, and content from members provides relevance and makes the network hard to leave. Both follow one rule: every claim shows who said it, when, and on what evidence.

Central · QS-produced

Evidence and interpretation

  • Signals: short, role-matched items, each ending with a "so what" and a suggested action
  • Data stories: drawn from public QS research (Global Student Flows, World Future Skills Index, rankings methodology), with a link to the source
  • Executive briefing: a monthly note per domain, written for vice-chancellors and their executive teams
  • Frameworks: market-prioritisation canvas, TNE partner evaluation, AI roadmap
  • Summit digests: what was said in the room, turned into actions

AI can help summarise and tag; an analyst signs off every item.

Member · user-generated

Practice and peer signal

  • Practice notes: "how we did it" write-ups by named leaders
  • Ask the network: questions answered by peers, with the best answers promoted
  • Pulse results: aggregated, anonymised operational data
  • Circle outputs: charters, shared templates, quarterly outputs
  • Partner content: always labelled "Partner", never mixed into QS editorial

Only verified posters, moderated by coordinators; members can report posts; no named data about prospective students.

07 · Connection to QS conferences & summits

Summits become the heartbeat of a year-round cycle

Today a summit is a peak of engagement followed by silence. The membership fills the gaps between summits, and the summits in turn become the membership's main way to recruit and renew.

Before · 6 weeks out

Prime

  • Suggested meetings between members, matched on their profiles
  • Pre-reads and Pulse questions for the summit
  • Circle meet-ups scheduled into the agenda
  • Partners invited to host themed roundtables
During

Convene

  • Member lounge and closed-door roundtables (Chatham House)
  • Live Pulse polls, with results shown in the room
  • Structured one-to-one meetings between members and partners
  • Delegates who aren't members get a 60-day trial
After · 2 weeks

Capture

  • Summit digest and recordings go into the library
  • Actions and introductions followed up by coordinators
  • Roundtable outputs published as practice notes
  • Trial users onboarded into the relevant Circles
Between summits

Sustain

  • Monthly Circle sessions and virtual roundtables
  • Signals and briefings keep the questions live
  • Challenge Board pilots run to results
  • Outputs brought back to the next summit stage
The commercial loop: summit delegates become members, and one-off summit sponsors become annual partners, because a summit sponsorship bundled with year-round membership access is worth more than either alone. Member outputs in turn give summit agendas more original, better-evidenced content, which strengthens QS's convening brand.

10 · Governance & trust

The Independence Charter

"Ranked-party-pays" is the recurring failure mode for any authority. The Chirikov (2022) finding about QS services is exactly the kind of challenge this product must not amplify. The charter is published and applies to members, partners and staff alike.

1 · No bearing on rankings

Membership, tier, Circle participation and partner status have no bearing on any QS ranking, survey or methodology decision. Rankings staff don't advise members on their own scores.

2 · Contributed data is ring-fenced

Pulse data is used only for member benchmarks unless separate, specific consent is given. It is never an input to rankings.

3 · Partner content is always labelled

Partner material is always marked and never presented as QS analysis. QS keeps editorial control of co-developed research.

4 · Members control contact

No partner contact without the member's opt-in. No personal data is sold. Introductions need both sides' consent.

5 · Every claim shows its source

Source, date, author type and cohort size are on every item. Illustrative or sample content is marked as such.

6 · Independent oversight

An annual review by a panel that includes independent members, with published findings.
